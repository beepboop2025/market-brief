# Free launch experiment

Market Brief starts as a funding-and-liquidity companion for broader market
research. The strongest external demand evidence is for asset research and
explanations of market moves. Whether people repeatedly want this narrower
context product is an open hypothesis, not a proven market.

## What to measure

An activated person completes a brief and uses a source link, copies a useful
brief, or returns for a comparison. Count these distinct events separately.
One HTTP fetch, a retry, a health check, a crawler, a test installation, or an
internal smoke test is not an activated user. A useful tool call is a
user-initiated or independently integrated production request that returns
usable context or a useful explicit gap, after excluding tests and retries.
Count clients, persons and applications separately; IP addresses and user-agent
strings do not identify distinct people reliably.

The initial app deliberately has no cross-site analytics or identity service.
We therefore cannot currently calculate global activated users or retention
from it. Measure genuine skill installation events from the directory as
installation evidence only. For early distribution tests, obtain explicit
opt-in pilot feedback and aggregate usage receipts from independent integrators;
do not manufacture traffic. Before making any aggregate MAU claim, implement
an appropriate privacy-preserving consent and deduplication design.

## A million is a unit, not a forecast

One million useful calls per month can be modeled as 10,000 active people
making 100 useful calls each, or 100 independent applications with 100 active
people each making 100 useful calls. Neither scenario establishes demand or
capacity. At one brief on each of 22 market days, roughly 45,455 active people
would be needed for one million briefs. The three source fetches inside one
brief count as one brief, not three users or three independent activations.

One million monthly active people is a much larger objective than one million
calls or cumulative installations. A large platform's feature adoption is not
transferable to a new standalone product. No million-user outcome, conversion
rate, growth rate, or completion date is guaranteed here.

## Test the habit before expanding

The first proposed gate is 20 independent users who complete two useful
sessions and five developer integrations that produce their own useful
briefs. These are target thresholds, not achieved results. Record why they
returned, what they could not answer, and whether the source links changed
their understanding. Separate research use from requests for personalized
trades that the product does not provide.

After a first cohort, test 100 activated users with an explicit seven-day
return measurement and a reviewed cost-per-useful-brief estimate. A suggested
continuation criterion is at least 25% returning in the second week and clear
reports of saved research time; this is a proposed product decision, not an
industry benchmark. If users mainly request ticker catalysts, prioritize a
lawfully sourced asset/event layer rather than adding more macro tiles.

Only after retention and source capacity are demonstrated should the launch
target 1,000 weekly active users and partnerships with existing copilots.
Record response latency, upstream failures, source ages, incomplete briefs,
and cost alongside usage. More requests with no useful evidence are not
success. Free hosting should be capacity-bounded before broad distribution.

## Distribution order

1. A no-account browser example and a versioned, one-command skill install.
2. Owner-maintained Claude and Codex marketplaces with verified consumer
   installs, plus a genuine skills.sh listing observation.
3. Concrete reviewed-directory submission packages including screenshots,
   repeatable positive/negative examples, privacy, terms, publisher identity,
   and truthful coverage. Account-owned verification and platform review are
   separate from owner-controlled publication.
4. Opt-in integration pilots in existing trading-research workflows. Outreach
   and public social messages require their own authorized recipients/channel;
   this launch does not send unsolicited messages or buy traffic.

Share actions are user-controlled and retain source URLs. There is no
automated posting, invented testimonial, referral spam, or installation loop.
Paid directories and campaigns are outside this zero-spend launch.

## Free terms

No payment collection or automatic paid conversion is enabled. The code is
MIT-licensed. Hosted early access remains free while product demand is tested;
future hosted changes would be announced explicitly. A compatible AI host may
charge for its own subscription independently. Free access alone is not the
product's differentiation; a useful, verifiable repeated workflow must earn
retention.
